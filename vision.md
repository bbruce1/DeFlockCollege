# Vision

## The bet

That the binding constraint on this kind of civic action is **friction, not
conviction**. There are far more students who object to plate readers around
their campus than students willing to run a club about it, and the gap between
those two numbers is made almost entirely of logistics that can be deleted.

If the bet is wrong, chapters will generate and go quiet, and the number that
matters will stay flat while the chapter count climbs.

## Why it spreads

**The marginal cost of a campus is flat.** One student, twenty minutes. That does
not rise at the two hundredth school, because nothing about the two hundredth
requires more of us than the first did.

**Nothing routes through us.** No approval queue, no intake call, no form a human
has to read. Growth is not capped by anyone's calendar, which means it is not
capped by our attention, our funding, or our burnout.

**It travels the way students already send things.** One person sends a link to a
friend at another school, and that friend has a chapter live the same day. That
is the existing behaviour of the audience, not a new one we are asking for.

**Density compounds inside a state.** One campus is a letter. Five in a state is a
problem a representative cannot file away, and the statewide reader count on
every page is there to make that argument without a sentence.

**The first action sits below the threshold of identity.** This is the core
insight. Joining a movement requires deciding you are the kind of person who
joins movements. Pressing a button that opens a written email does not. A student
can act first and decide what it meant later. Most organising has this backwards.

## Measure the right thing

**Chapter count is a vanity metric.** It will look good early and mean little.

The number that decides whether this works is **outreach emails actually sent**,
per campus, over time. Two hundred pages nobody uses is a worse outcome than
fifteen moving a city council, and only one of those numbers tells you which you
have. Instrument it from the first commit; it cannot be retrofitted onto a
hundred chapters of missing history.

## What the page must always say

Three things appear on every chapter, exempt from any randomisation:

1. **Make the Instagram.** The generator builds the site; it cannot build an
   audience. It is the one thing we ask a creator to do themselves.
2. **Emails and votes. Never vandalism.** Damaging a camera loses the argument,
   hands opponents their story, and in many states costs a student the vote they
   came to use.
3. **Students. Not the school.** Named in the page's own voice, at full size,
   never as fine print.

## Design principles

**Show before you tell.** A count is abstract. A dial proving that 67% of the
horizon around campus is covered by cameras facing outward is the same fact,
arrived at visually. Components that render real data are worth more than
components that assert things.

**Every number must be checkable.** Counts come from public map data. If one is
wrong it is wrong in a way anyone can verify and correct, and one exaggerated
figure discredits every accurate one.

**Never claim what the data does not show.** A campus with no mapped readers gets
a page that says so and invites mapping, not one that asserts a ring that is not
there. Coverage is community-contributed, so a count is a floor and never a
census.

**Nothing may hide behind an animation.** If a script fails, an observer never
fires, or a frame drops, every value must already be correct on the page. This
has been the most repeated bug in the project's history.

**A recognisable network.** The hero is identical on every chapter, word for word.
A student who has seen one should recognise the next instantly, because that
recognition is the growth mechanism. Variation lives below it, never in it.

## Risk posture

The realistic worst case is a cease-and-desist from a school, answered by taking
that chapter down the same day. That is an afternoon, not an existential event,
and the ability to do it quickly is what makes the open model defensible.

Three things keep it there:

- **Collect almost nothing.** No accounts, no tracking, no profiles. Ownership is
  an email address, held in a form that cannot be reversed.
- **Names, never logos.** A school's name identifies which campus a chapter is
  about. Its logo, seal, or mascot would imply sponsorship.
- **Every chapter removable within a day**, on request, without debate.

## Open questions

- **Officials sourcing.** Preloading the offices that can remove readers is the
  one part whose cost does not obviously stay flat. If a human has to research
  each school, the central thesis breaks. This is the highest-risk unknown.
- **Whether generated outreach gets read.** Identical form letters are easy for an
  office to discount. A required line in the student's own words is the current
  answer, and it is untested.
- **Whether a quiet chapter can be reclaimed** without inventing the moderation
  burden this design exists to avoid.
