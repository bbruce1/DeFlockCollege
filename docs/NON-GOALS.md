# Non-goals

What DeFlockCollege deliberately does not build. Each of these is a thing a reasonable
person will propose, and each one, if adopted, converts a twenty-minute action back into
an organizing project.

## No meetings

No recurring meeting, no attendance, no room booking. A meeting is a weekly obligation
that only the most committed student will carry, and it filters out everyone whose
objection to campus ALPR cameras is real but not yet identity-level.

## No chartering

No student-government registration, no constitution, no officer slate. Chartering puts the
chapter's existence at the mercy of an approval body with its own calendar — and gives the
school an administrative lever over a site that is explicitly not affiliated with it.

## No faculty advisor

No advisor to recruit, and no adult whose willingness gates whether the chapter exists.

## No logistics

No treasury, no dues, no merch, no event planning, no member roster. Each of these is a job
someone has to keep doing.

## Nothing routes through us

No approval step, no intake call, no waiting on a human to read a form. The moment any
part of chapter creation requires our attention, the marginal cost of a campus stops being
flat and growth is capped by our calendar again — which is the exact failure mode this
project exists to avoid.

## We do not send on the student's behalf

Emails open pre-written and pre-addressed, and the student presses send. Automating the
send would be faster and would destroy the thing that makes the message count.

---

## When to revisit

These are constraints for reaching the first hundred campuses, not permanent principles.
The signal that one of them should be reconsidered is a chapter where students are already
doing the thing on their own and the lack of structure is actively getting in their way —
not a chapter that has gone quiet. A quiet chapter is a demand problem, and adding
structure to it will not help.
